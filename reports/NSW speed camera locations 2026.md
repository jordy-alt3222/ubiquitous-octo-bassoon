# Official data maps about 280 NSW cameras today

About **280 of the roughly 408 fixed and red-light speed camera locations operating in NSW in 2026** can go into a Highway Radar ExCam file now with official coordinates. The rest cannot yet. Point-to-point (average speed) start and end cameras have no published coordinates, mobile camera sites are published as street and suburb names only, and the official TfNSW map the user named is behind a CloudFront block (HTTP 403) that this session did not try to get around. The only official coordinates come from Transport for NSW's Open Data Hub GPS workbook dated **November 2021**, licensed CC BY 4.0 ([TfNSW Open Data](https://opendata.transport.nsw.gov.au/dataset/nsw-speed-cameras)). The only official source that is current for 2026 is Revenue NSW's penalty-notice data, which runs to 30 September 2026 and gives each camera's road, suburb and enforced direction, but no coordinates ([Revenue NSW](https://www.revenue.nsw.gov.au/help-centre/resources-library/statistics)). Matching the two by road and suburb names gives coordinates to 105 of 138 active fixed-camera codes and 177 of 270 active red-light codes. The posted speed limit for each camera can be taken from TfNSW's Speed Zones layer, updated 28 October 2026. So an accurate file is possible for most fixed and red-light cameras. The gaps are about 80 cameras with no coordinates (mostly tunnels and intersections added after 2021), all 32 average-speed lengths and every mobile site. Each needs either a manual export from the official map in a normal browser or a data request to TfNSW.

## The official map is blocked, but its data has public twins

The page the user named, `transport.nsw.gov.au/.../speed-camera-locations-map`, returned HTTP 403 to every automated request. So did the related TfNSW PDFs for mobile, red-light, school-zone and average-speed cameras ([TfNSW map page](https://www.transport.nsw.gov.au/roadsafety/topics-tips/speeding/enforcement-cameras/speed-camera-locations-map)). Service NSW's "Check a speed camera location" page links to that map. It describes the map as covering fixed, school-zone fixed, mobile and red-light cameras, but it exposes no data endpoint ([Service NSW](https://www.service.nsw.gov.au/referral/check-a-speed-camera-location)). Nobody could see which feed the map loads. Opening it in a normal browser with DevTools → Network, and filtering for "FeatureServer" or "json", is the fastest way to find out.

Two public copies of what is probably the same data were reachable. The first is the Open Data Hub dataset `nsw-speed-cameras`, licensed CC BY, with metadata last modified 2023-08-21. It holds a November 2021 GPS workbook, fixed and red-light CSVs, and a January 2022 mobile CSV ([CKAN record](https://opendata.transport.nsw.gov.au/data/api/3/action/package_show?id=nsw-speed-cameras)). The workbook lists **102 fixed-camera site rows (125 cameras, 39 of them school-zone sites)** and **201 red-light site rows (222 cameras, 12 school-zone sites)**. Its columns are `SZ?, SUBURB/TOWN, ROAD/S, Cameras, Lat(n), Long(n)`, with up to three coordinate pairs per row. The second is an ArcGIS feature service on the NSW spatial portal, `TFNSW_Speed_Cameras_public`, which needs no login. It has three layers (fixed, fixed school-zone and red-light), every record is stamped 2021-05-27, and it matches the Open Data Hub CSV to within 0.0001° ([FeatureServer](https://portal.data.nsw.gov.au/arcgis/rest/services/Hosted/TFNSW_Speed_Cameras_public/FeatureServer?f=json)). Its catalogue record gives the licence as "not specified", so build from the Open Data Hub files. Their licence lets you redistribute the data on the condition that "Transport for NSW is attributed as the source" ([ODH licence](https://opendata.transport.nsw.gov.au/datalicence)).

The two sources that look relevant but are not should be kept out of the file. Geoplex, a private GIS firm, publishes ArcGIS items with 103 fixed cameras last edited in September 2020. They are unofficial and carry no licence ([Geoplex FS](https://services.arcgis.com/y3GIulxK3Ox4lXG0/arcgis/rest/services/NSW_Speed_Cameras_GPS_Locations/FeatureServer/0?f=json)). The API endpoint `api.transport.nsw.gov.au/v1/live/cameras` serves Live Traffic **traffic-view** CCTV cameras, not enforcement cameras ([Live Traffic Cameras](https://opendata.transport.nsw.gov.au/dataset/live-traffic-cameras)).

## Revenue NSW data brings the 2021 coordinates up to date

The 2021 GPS file is five years old, so it needs a current list to check against. Revenue NSW's `dfosf-001` penalty-notice files fill that role. They have one row per camera location code, offence and month, and they cover offences up to 30 September 2026. The `LOCATION_DETAILS` field is defined as "the street and suburb of a Speed, Red light… or Point to point camera, and the direction in which it operates" ([Revenue NSW notes](https://www.revenue.nsw.gov.au/help-centre/resources-library/statistics/penalty-notice-data-notes-dfosf-001-notes.xlsx)). A code counts as active here if it issued notices in July–September 2026. On that test there are **138 active fixed-camera codes and 270 active red-light codes**. There are more codes than 2021 site rows because each code is one camera or one intersection approach with its own direction.

The researchers matched these codes to 2021 rows by road and suburb names. The results show what can be used now:

| Camera type (active codes) | Exact or probable 2021 match (coordinates usable) | Ambiguous or possible (needs checking) | No 2021 row (no official coordinates) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fixed digital speed (138) | **105** | 3 | 30 |
| Red-light speed (270) | **177** | 44 | 49 |

These counts come from the researchers' master table, which joins Revenue NSW codes to the TfNSW November 2021 coordinates ([Revenue NSW](https://www.revenue.nsw.gov.au/help-centre/resources-library/statistics); [TfNSW Open Data](https://opendata.transport.nsw.gov.au/data/dataset/fb34bd89-443a-448c-a4a5-7c8caab70c44)).

Most of the unmatched fixed cameras are **tunnel cameras**. They are active in 2026 but were never in the 2021 GPS file: Cross City Tunnel, Eastern Distributor, Lane Cove Tunnel, M5 East, Sydney Harbour Tunnel, NorthConnex, WestConnex M4 East and M8, and four Rozelle Interchange codes added in early 2024. Other new fixed sites show up in the penalty data at the Grand Parade in Brighton-Le-Sands and Sans Souci (from December 2023 to February 2024) and on the Pacific Highway at Cowan (from August 2025). Hornsby Council's notice said the Cowan camera "will be operational in May 2025" ([Hornsby Council notice](https://www.hornsby.nsw.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0007/283498/Fixed-Speed-Camera-Installation-Old-Pac-Hwy-community-notification-April-2025.pdf)). Revenue NSW shows **49 red-light codes first issuing notices after July 2022**. TfNSW media releases confirm several of them, including Milperra Road at Nancy Ellis Leebold Drive (February 2024), Punchbowl Road at King Georges Road, Wiley Park (July 2024), Pennant Hills Road at the M1, Wahroonga (June 2025) and Lane Cove Road at Twin Road, North Ryde (August 2025) ([TfNSW, Milperra](https://www.transport.nsw.gov.au/news-and-events/media-releases/dangerous-driving-given-red-light-milperra-1); [TfNSW, Wahroonga](https://www.transport.nsw.gov.au/news-and-events/media-releases/dangerous-driving-given-red-light-wahroonga)). Those releases name the intersections, so geocoding the new sites is straightforward. The resulting coordinates would still be derived, not official.

Some 2021 sites should be dropped. Eleven fixed rows and eight red-light rows have no notices at all from 2022 to 2026. Among them are the Pacific Highway sites at Hungry Head, Valla Beach, Korora, New Italy and North Macksville, which sit on alignments the highway upgrade has bypassed. They are probably decommissioned, although no official notice was found ([Revenue NSW](https://www.revenue.nsw.gov.au/help-centre/resources-library/statistics)). One 2021 row has a clear error: the red-light row "Albury – Borella Road and Young Street" carries coordinates in inner Sydney (-33.8792, 151.1957), so exclude it. A month with no notices does not prove a camera was switched off, so treat the "inactive" flags as signals to check, not as proof.

## School zones need a flag the ExCam format lacks

School-zone cameras appear as fixed or red-light cameras with `SZ?`=Y in the 2021 data, or with "(Z)" in their Revenue NSW location names: **39 fixed sites (59 cameras) and 12 red-light sites (14 cameras)** in the 2021 data ([TfNSW Open Data](https://opendata.transport.nsw.gov.au/data/dataset/fb34bd89-443a-448c-a4a5-7c8caab70c44)). The NSW Government says these cameras "operate 24 hours per day, seven days a week" and also enforce the **40 km/h school-zone limit** during sign-posted school-zone times ([NSW Government](https://www.nsw.gov.au/driving-boating-and-transport/driving-nsw/roads-safety-and-rules/safe-driving/speed-limits-and-cameras/fixed-and-mobile-speed-cameras)). No large expansion from 2022 to 2026 was found. The 2025 "smart school zone sign" trial at Wallsend South does not enforce anything: "they will not capture or store any images" ([NSW Government](https://www.nsw.gov.au/ministerial-releases/nsw-leads-way-nation-first-trial-of-smart-school-zone-signs-%E2%80%93-technology-pioneered-hunter)). The ExCam `flg` bitmask has no school-zone bit. So `spd` should hold the normal posted limit, and the school-zone fact can go into `str` (for example by appending "(school zone 40)"), unless the app supports a time-dependent limit.

## Average-speed lengths are known by name but not by coordinate

TfNSW's enforcement map, as copied by speedcam.com.au (checked 20 October 2026), lists **32 lengths in operation, numbered 1–33 with no number 19**. Nine more are at planning stage, including the M7, M5, Eastern Distributor, Cross City Tunnel and Sydney Harbour Tunnel ([speedcam.com.au](https://www.speedcam.com.au/blog/average-speed-cameras-nsw/)). **Only two lengths fine all vehicles**: No. 8, Hume Highway Coolac–Gundagai (16 km), and No. 18, Pacific Highway Kew–Lake Innes (15 km). Both have done so since 1 July 2025 under the light-vehicle trial ([Towards Zero, TfNSW](https://collaborationhub.towardszero.nsw.gov.au/news-activities/news/average-speed-camera-light-vehicle-trial-enforcement-began-1-july-2025)). Enforcement continued after the trial's scheduled end on 30 June 2026, pending an independent review. Revenue NSW data confirms it: "Average Speed Camera – Light" notices are issued only at Coolac NB, Gundagai SB, Kew SB and Lake Innes NB, and they continue into FY 2026-27 ([Revenue NSW DSF010](https://www.revenue.nsw.gov.au/help-centre/resources-library/statistics/all-speeding-and-red-light-camera-detected-offences-dsf010.xlsx)). The other 30 lengths enforce only heavy vehicles over 4.5 t. Most were set up in 2010–2015. The newer ones are WestConnex M8, M5 East, M4 Tunnel and NorthConnex (2020), the M4-M8 Link (January 2023), Rozelle Interchange (March 2024), M4 Eastern Creek–South Penrith (March 2025) and M2 Naremburn–Baulkham Hills (October 2025). The former No. 19, Urunga–Valla, shows notices only up to 2021 and should be left out.

The problem for the app is that **no official coordinates exist for any start or end camera**. The Open Data Hub has no average-speed dataset ([CKAN search](https://opendata.transport.nsw.gov.au/data/api/3/action/package_search?q=average%20speed)), the TfNSW map PDF returned 403, and OpenStreetMap's Overpass API was unreachable. The Revenue NSW location names do give direction and place for each length's end camera, such as "Pacific Highway Lake Innes Northbound". So a later geocoding pass knows what to look for. The ExCam bits 2 and 3 (start and end of an average-speed section) cannot be filled with authoritative points today. Light-vehicle users would care most about lengths 8 and 18, so those are the ones to map first.

## Mobile sites are street names, never points

The official mobile list is a TfNSW PDF, `crs_mobile_speed_cameras.pdf`, which now sits under a `/documents/2026/` path. It is ordered by suburb and street, and a dealer blog describes it as about 53 pages, "typically updated every few months" ([Tynan Motors](https://www.tynan.com.au/blog/latest-sydney-nsw-mobile-speed-camera-locations-december-2025)). It returned 403, so no authoritative 2026 count exists. The only machine-readable official copy is the January 2022 CSV: **1,166 rows with only `Suburb, Street` columns and no coordinates**. Many rows group several suburbs together ([Mobile CSV](https://opendata.transport.nsw.gov.au/data/dataset/fb34bd89-443a-448c-a4a5-7c8caab70c44/resource/b4e0c74e-10a6-48e8-8421-2cd0294af6ae/download/mobile-speed-camera-locations-january-2022.csv)). That list is now out of date: in April 2024 the government approved **2,700 new mobile sites**, 1,650 of them regional, while holding enforcement at about 21,000 hours a month ([NSW Government](https://www.nsw.gov.au/media-releases/nsw-government-zeroes-on-road-safety)). Mobile cameras have carried warning signs again since April 2023: two before the vehicle and one after ([NSW Government](https://www.nsw.gov.au/media-releases/return-of-warning-signs-for-mobile-speed-cameras)). Phone and seatbelt detection trailers are a separate program, and their locations are not published.

A mobile entry names a whole road, sometimes a 40 km route, not a camera position, so any point placed on it would be invented. Mobile data belongs in a separate "risk road" layer if Highway Radar supports one, not in the point file.

## How each ExCam field can be filled

| ExCam field | Fixed and red-light (matched) | Source and confidence |
|---|---|---|
| `lat`, `lon` | One record per `Lat(n)/Long(n)` pair | TfNSW, November 2021; official but dated |
| `flg` | 1 for fixed; 3 (speed + red light) for red-light speed cameras | From which 2021 sheet or Revenue NSW camera type the record comes from |
| `dir` | Cardinal direction from Revenue NSW (N/S/E/W), converted to a bearing; omit for two-way codes | Official direction, but the bearing is approximate. Snapping to the road geometry is better. At two-camera rows, the data does not say which point serves which direction |
| `spd` | Speed Zones polyline within 30 m | Derived from TfNSW Speed Zones, October 2026 ([Speed Zones](https://opendata.transport.nsw.gov.au/dataset/speed-zones)). At intersections the cross street can be caught, so check it |
| `str` | `ROAD/S` cut at ", between", plus suburb | Official text |

None of the camera sources gives a speed limit or a bearing. Both are derived values, and the file's metadata should say so, together with the required credit: "Contains data from Transport for NSW, licensed under CC BY 4.0."

## Conclusion

The best data for 2026 is not one dataset but a join of two: Revenue NSW's penalty data shows which cameras are operating, and TfNSW's 2021 GPS file says where they are. Neither is enough alone. The 2021 file would place cameras at five bypassed Pacific Highway sites and one in the wrong city, and it would miss every tunnel. The penalty data has no coordinates. Every remaining gap (about 80 fixed and red-light codes, 32 average-speed lengths with roughly 64 end points, and the mobile list) points back to the TfNSW map that this session could not open. One manual export from that map in a browser, or one email to opendataprogram@transport.nsw.gov.au asking for a current GPS file, would close more of the gap than any further automated research. Until then, a file built from the matched 280 records is accurate for most fixed and red-light cameras in NSW, but it has no average-speed sections and no tunnel cameras.
